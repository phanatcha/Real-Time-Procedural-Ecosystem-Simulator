- **47, juvenile growth:** an animal born in the simulation starts at **Newborn Size Fraction** (0.4) of its adult size, speed, vision and feeding reach and grows to full size by its maturity time (`SeekFood.UpdateGrowth`). Growing costs energy in proportion to the body mass put on: **Growth Energy Cost** (0.5) times the adult body's own meat (Carcass Body Energy × body mass), spread over the youth, so maturing sooner is a shorter but costlier youth instead of a free advantage (item 9's note). A young animal has the mass of its current size, so it leaves a small carcass and costs less to run while small. The body, its parts and its upkeep follow in 5% steps. Founders placed by a scene and animals back from off-screen numbers are full-grown. The genes keep the adult values; species averages of feeding reach and body mass now include the young.

Each child counts as a birth. A later child with nowhere to be born isn't born and its share stays with the parent. Shown on the panel's largest-species line and in `traits.csv`. ## Backlog item 27, thirst — 1 November 2026, third round

- Every animal has hydration (`maxHydration` 100) that drains 0.4/s in comfort, plus as much again per unit of heat stress, so a full animal lasts about four minutes. Below `thirstThreshold` (50%) it is thirsty; with none left it loses `thirstDamagePerSecond` (2) health and dies of a new cause, **thirst**.
- Fresh water is the terrain's rivers and lakes (`riverStrength` or `lakeStrength` on the sample); the sea is salt. A thirsty animal walks rays out to its vision radius every two seconds and takes the first water each meets (the shore), picks the nearest fresh one, and asks the NavMesh for a spot at its edge it can stand or wade in (`SeekFood.TryFindDrinkingSpot`). Spots that can't be reached are remembered for 60 s, as unreachable food is.
- A **Drink** goal in `UtilityDecisionPolicy` competes with food and mates: worth `thirstValue` (1) × the animal's energy store when dry, nothing at the threshold, less travel, so a dying animal goes to water before food. Two states, seeking water and drinking; drinking refills at 25/s until 95%, and any animal standing in fresh water drinks for free. Newborns are born hydrated. Off-screen populations ignore thirst.
- Deaths by thirst are in the telemetry records, the season summaries, the telemetry log and `population.csv` (schema 3, a `thirst` column before `other`). The inspector shows water beside energy.
- Five-year soaks: FOREST-001 14 thirst deaths of 376 (starvation 305), average 36 animals; MOSSY-FJORD 42 of 873 (starvation 668), average 83. Thirst is a steady minor cause, 4 to 5% of deaths, a little higher in winter when animals range for buried food; populations are unchanged.
- Two Play Mode tests: a thirsty animal walks to a stand-in water point and drinks until nearly full (`SeekFood.DrinkingWaterLocator` supplies water where there is no terrain); with no water, an animal with no hydration dies of thirst and the death is counted. The first run caught a real bug: an animal stopped drinking the moment it crossed the thirst threshold, so a drink now keeps a quarter of its value until the animal is nearly full. Full Unity suite: **279 passed, 0 failed, 0 skipped** (`Logs/TestResults/20261101T165334883659Z/results.xml`).

## Backlog item 58, selection on each gene — 13 November 2026

- **The tool:** `Tools/selection_gradients.py <runs or comparison folder>` reads each run's `lives.csv` (item 57) and measures, for every trait (harmful mutations, every perk, every gene, and the total size of each kind of body part), how strongly it predicts the number of young an animal left. Animals born in the simulation that have died are grouped into cohorts by the year and season of their birth; within each cohort fitness is made relative and each trait standardised. It prints the selection differential S (total selection, including what a trait picks up from traits it travels with) and the Lande–Arnold gradient β (direct selection, from a multiple regression on all traits at once), with a 95% range, overall and for each season. Runs with the same experiment label are pooled. Pure Python, no packages; a 25-year, three-run pool takes about 5 s.
- **Honest ranges:** relatives are alike, so ordinary regression ranges are too narrow. The standard errors are clustered by first parent (brothers and sisters), and the do-nothing marker genes and marker perk set the floor: "clearly selected" lists only traits whose range excludes zero and whose |β| is above every marker's. `--place` holds the birthplace equal (a smooth surface in the birth position within each cohort), because family lines live in different places and a line in a rich patch leaves more young whatever its genes. `--fitness bred` counts only young that went on to breed.
- **Checked on made-up records with a known answer** (12,000 animals): a gene with direct selection of +0.3 came out at +0.29; a gene that only travelled with it (r = 0.7) had S +0.20 but β −0.01; a gene pushed up in winter and down in summer came out at +0.28 and −0.30 by season; a planted cost of 5% per harmful mutation came out at 5.4%; a perk planted on low-load animals had S +0.02 and β −0.01.

## Backlog item 67, bigger bodies last longer between meals — 13 November 2026

- **The rule:** with **Scale With Body Size** on (the default), an animal's body mass from its genes, against the founders' (bulk squared, times height at a 0.35 share, as before), sets two things. ## Backlog items 78 and 79, natural events and wildfire — 14 November 2026

- **78, natural events:** `NaturalEventDirector` (new, in `Assets/Animals/Scripts/NaturalEvents`, added to any scene with a Species Manager, as the god tools are) keeps the weather natural events read, rolls for random starts every 5 simulated seconds, runs the events and records each start, its progress every 30 s and its end with the Species Manager, so they are in `events.csv`. A kind of event is a `NaturalEventKind` that says how often it starts under the conditions of the moment (season, temperature, dryness, wind) and makes `NaturalEvent`s; the wildfire is the first, and a drought, flood or storm would each be one more registered kind. The weather (`NaturalWeather`) is a dryness index that follows the season with a lag of about a month (warm, dry summers raise it, snowmelt and snow keep spring and winter at nothing, dormant cured grass adds to it), plus seeded dry and wet spells, and a wind that wanders around a prevailing south-westerly. At the seasons' full strength dryness peaks at 0.62 in late summer and 0.58 in early fall, and is under 0.15 from late fall to late spring. **Random Events** off, or `run_soak.py --natural-events 0`, leaves only god tools to start events.
- **79, wildfire:** `WildfireGrid` (pure C#) keeps 8 m fire cells over the island. Fuel is read from the terrain at the fire front as it arrives: load from tree cover and grass biomass (less where rock shows), and fuel moisture from the ground's moisture, the dryness, rivers, lakes and shores, and snow. Water, bare rock and snow don't burn. Each burning cell flares up and dies down over its burn (6 s for grass, 20 s for dense forest) and passes fire to each of its eight neighbours with a chance set over the whole burn by the neighbour's flammability and its own peak heat, raised downwind (more for crown fires) and uphill, lowered at night (45%); so grass carries fire fast and forest slowly. In a strong wind (above 7 m/s) burning crowns throw embers that start spot fires 3 to 9 cells downwind. A fire ends when nothing burns. Smoke is kept in 32 m blocks, given off by the burning cells, blown downwind, spread and thinned. Lightning strikes land on fuel within 500 m of a random animal, at **Strikes Per Year** (45) times the fire danger (dryness squared, times 0.4 to 1 for warmth, rarely above 0.25), and catch with four times the fuel's flammability as the chance; none in a world's first year (**First Fire After**, 400 s), while the seasons ease in and the founders settle.
- **Animals:** near flames an animal loses up to **Fire Damage Per Second** (30) health at full heat, which falls off over about 12 m from burning ground, a tenth as much in water, and dies of a new cause, **Fire**. Smoke drains up to 6 stamina a second, stops stamina recovering and adds up to half again to upkeep (through the normal upkeep, so the energy ledger still balances). An animal notices a fire within 150 m by its smoke and glow; the utility policy weighs getting away (worth up to twice its energy store, more the nearer the fire and more downwind of it) against food, water and mates, and above a danger of 0.45 runs from it before anything else, predators included, turning across the wind when downwind of the fire, using the existing Fleeing state. Flames break off a courtship; food in burning cells is left alone. An animal in water is mostly safe. The inspector shows "running from a fire", the heat and the smoke.

## Backlog item 65, family lines as a Muller plot — 14 November 2026

- **The lines:** a family line is a founder's descendants, or a branch inside a line that grew big: one whose descendants alive were at some time at least 10% of all the animals, while the line it grew in had at least 10% of all the animals outside it at some time the branch lived (so a branch that is just its whole line again isn't a line of its own). A branch that began with a perk gain is a line once it reaches 3%, drawn striped. Lines follow first parents, the parent that led a courtship for a child of two. Shares count only at samples with at least 20 animals alive, so the founders' first few children don't each look like a takeover; at most 24 branch lines are kept, raising the 10% until they fit. The same rules run in C# (`FamilyLineChart`, `Assets/Animals/Scripts/FamilyLines.cs`) and Python (`Tools/muller_plot.py`), each checked on the same made-up family with a known answer.
- **In the game:** M shows the family lines panel (`FamilyLinesView`): each line a band as tall as its share of the animals, nested inside the band it grew from (its own animals half below and half above the lines inside it, as Muller plots draw them), from the world's start to now, with a tick each year; perk lines striped; below, the biggest lines now, with their share now, their peak and the perks most of them carry. It keeps every animal's life from the Species Manager as animals join and die, and redraws every 2 s while shown. A line that took over the whole of its parent line is listed with it, as a chain ("F3 → L6 → L15"). Families keep their founder's hue. The soak runner saves the panel as `family-lines.png`, with its legend in `family-lines.txt`.
- **From a recording:** `Tools/muller_plot.py <run, recording or folder of runs>` reads `lives.csv` (any recording since schema 8) and writes `muller.svg`, `muller_lines.csv` (each line's parent, first animal, birth, peak, share at the end and perk shares at its peak) and the two tables ggmuller reads, `muller_edges.csv` (Parent, Identity, with a root `0` above the founders' lines) and `muller_populations.csv` (Generation in simulated years, Identity, each line's own animals), so `get_Muller_df(edges, pop_df)` draws the same plot in R. It prints every line and when each branch first held half of its parent line and half of all the animals. Options set the shares, the perks that start lines and the sample step (40 s); `--self-test` checks the rules. A 25-year run takes under a second.
- **What the recorded runs show** (26 runs from items 60, 62 and 68):
  1. **A founder's line takes over, but slowly.** In the sixteen ten-year runs (items 62 and 68) one founder's line held half the animals by year 1.6 to 8.2 (by year 2 in nine of them), but had all of them within ten years in only four (by years 5.5 to 7.8); in the other twelve two founders' lines still shared the island at the end (the larger with 54 to 87%). In the six 25-year runs of clones and mate-first (item 60) one founder's line held every animal by year 3.4 to 19.7. So the "one founder's line taking over in the first years" the item expected happens as a majority, not yet as a takeover.
  2. **Takeovers come as chains.** Inside the winning line, branch after branch took over the one before, each a line in its own right: in item 60's `clones-r1`, F3's line passed through eight nested takeovers, to `L1234`, born in year 6.2, then `L1608` and `L3238` (which began with a regeneration gain) took over in turn. In a population that mostly clones that is what a selective sweep, or drift, looks like.
  3. **Item 62's camouflage sweep shows as one striped band.** In `asymmetric-r4` a line that began with camouflage in year 3.5 reached 73% of the animals.
  4. **Perk lines ride along.** Lines that began with a thick fur gain grew to 10 to 100% of the animals, but so did lines that began with the marker perk, which does nothing (to 100% in `clones-r1` and item 60's `mate-first-r2`, 45% in item 62's `symmetric-r3`). A perk line's size alone says nothing about selection; read it beside the marker lines and the selection gradients (item 58).
- **Checked in a soak:** a fresh five-year MOSSY-FJORD soak saved the panel as `family-lines.png` (`Logs/SoakRuns/MOSSY-FJORD-20261113T193606Z`): 31 lines in three founders' families, F1's line at 64% of the animals at the end. The tool, sampling the recording at the same 160 times, found the same 31 lines, every one in the panel's legend among them. The panel hasn't been looked at in the Editor.

## Backlog item 59, replaying a run without selection — 14 November 2026

- **The replay:** `NeutralReplay` (`Assets/Animals/Scripts/NeutralReplay.cs`, pure code with no scene) replays a recording's `lives.csv`. Every animal keeps its place in time, born when it was born and gone when it died, so the head count, the age structure and the seasons are the real run's. But each birth takes its parents at random from the animals alive then, among those within a quarter of the real parent's age (at least 5 s either side), and the young get their genes from those parents by the game's own rules (`AnimalGenome.Recombine` and `CreateChild`). A litter keeps its shared parents, a child of two parents gets two, and a clone gets one. Who lives, who breeds and how many young they have no longer depend on genes, so a trait that moves as far in the replays moved by drift or a bias in the mutation rules, not by adaptation. `--mode tree` keeps the real family tree instead and draws only the mutations afresh down it. That gives exact demography and generations, but finds less, because selection shaped the tree.
- **Outside Unity:** `Tools/replay` is a small dotnet 10 console project that compiles the game's `AnimalGenome.cs` and `NeutralReplay.cs` with a stand-in for the bits of `UnityEngine` they use (`UnityShim.cs`). `Tools/neutral_replay.py <run, recording or comparison folder>` builds it (output in the system temp folder, never in the repository), reads each run's mutation settings from its soak log (perk gain and loss, harmful mutation chance; a log without the perk line predates item 62's 1% each way), and runs 39 replays per run. It writes `neutral/replays-<mode>.csv` and a chart per trait, `neutral/neutral-<mode>.svg`: the real run as a blue line over the replays' band. It prints each trait's start, end and band, and for runs pooled by experiment how many ended above and below the band against chance. A recording takes about 5 s.
- **The band is exact:** all but the 2 highest and 2 lowest of 39 replays, so a neutral trait's real value ends above it with chance 2/40 = 5%, and below it the same. A first version used the 5th to 95th percentile of 20 replays. That puts a neutral trait outside about 19% of the time, not 10%, and made random parents look too narrow; the markers caught it.
- **Fair on the markers:** over the 56 recorded runs with life records (items 60, 62, 67 and 68, and the single soaks of 13 November), the do-nothing markers (item 56) ended outside the band 25 times in 224 with random parents and 22 in 224 keeping the family tree, against 22.4 by chance. Random-parent replays ran a median 5% more generations than the real runs (−9% to +26%), so their bands are if anything a little wide.
- **What the replays show** (random parents, runs that ended above or below the band; chance puts 5% of runs on each side; "beyond chance" means p below 0.01 for that many runs on one side):

  | Experiment | Beyond chance | Not beyond chance |
  |---|---|---|
  | Item 60, clones, 3 × 25 years | **Neck** above in 3 of 3, **thick fur** 3 of 3, height and night vision 2 of 3; venom below 2 of 3 | Harmful mutations below in 1 of 3 |
  | Item 60, mate first, 3 × 25 years | **Height** and **speed** above in 2 of 3; **diet** lower (more plants) and **harmful mutations** below in 2 of 3 | Neck, thick fur |
  | Item 62, old perk rule, 4 × 10 years | **Neck** above in 3 of 4 | Everything else |
  | Item 62, new perk rule, 4 × 10 years | Nothing | |
  | Item 67, size scaling, 4 × 20 years | Nothing | Bulk below in 2 of 4 (p 0.014), harmful mutations below in 2 of 4 |
  | Item 67, old rule, 4 × 20 years | Nothing | Bulk inside in all 4 |
  | Item 68, with and without ageing, 4 × 10 years each | Nothing | Ageing onset inside the band in all 4 runs with ageing |

  Sexual drive comes out beyond chance in every item 60 run, but only because those runs locked it (the replays don't), so it is not read here.
- **What it shows:**
  1. **Necks are the clearest adaptation so far.** They grew beyond anything chance gives in every long clone run and three of four ten-year runs, as item 58's gradient said (tall forest food, item 8).
  2. **Thick fur is favoured in the long runs.** It rose beyond chance in all three 25-year clone runs, while no ten-year pool showed it. That fits item 58's winter gradient, and corrects item 62's reading of thick fur's share as no better than chance: ten years is too short to see it.
  3. **Mating purges the load.** Harmful mutations ended below the band in two of three mate-first runs and one of three clone runs, as item 60 found.
  4. **Item 67's fall in bulk points the same way but is short of the bar on its own:** bulk ended below every replay but two in two of four size-scaling runs (p 0.014) and in none of the old-rule runs. The gradient (item 58, β −0.031) is the stronger test of the two.
  5. **Most genes move only as far as chance moves them** in ten- to twenty-year runs, which is what item 58's weak gradients predicted.

## Backlog item 76, plankton at night and bioluminescence — 14 November 2026

Phase 6 of the day and night plan (items 70 to 77), as agreed on 3 November.

- **Plankton rise at night:** plankton food (the open sea's) is worth half its nutrition by day, one and a half times on a moonless night and 1.1 times under a full moon high in the sky, and in between through twilight (`SeaFoodRules.PlanktonAtSurface`, `FoodItem.CurrentNutrition`, which both the animals' food choice and eating use). With day and night off it is worth its nutrition as before. Over a year it averages about its nutrition: more in the long winter nights, less in summer.
- **A sixth perk, glow** (bioluminescence, `AnimalPerk.Glow`, last in the list so saved genomes keep their perks), gained and lost like the others (0.2% and 2% per birth). In the dark a glowing animal is noticed as a partner from up to twice as far, and spotted by any animal from up to half as far again; camouflage stops working on it at night. Both follow the darkness (1 − daylight), so by day the glow does nothing but cost. It costs 4% more energy. While any animal glows, animals search the animals around them out to the range a glowing one could be seen from. `SeekFood.SetPerk` gives or takes a perk on a live animal (for god tools and experiments), and the soak runner's `--founder-perks Glow` starts the founders with it.

At night the open sea sparkles where animals swim through it (`SeaSparkle`, one shared particle system with `Hidden/Boreal/SeaSparkle`: a few blue flashes per metre swum, more the darker it is, only within 220 m of the camera).
